**A key principle of democracy is that ______.**

- A. the minority may vote but the majority decides
- B. only the majority vote is counted
- C. legal sovereignty is unquestionable
- D. an electoral college is always necessary

**Key: A** — Democracy combines majority rule with the right of the minority to participate and dissent. The majority’s decision prevails, but minority votes are counted and minority rights protected.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q1 (key corrected, wording repaired)*

**Sovereignty that rests on the consent and mandate of the people is called ______.**

- A. external sovereignty
- B. popular sovereignty
- C. legal sovereignty
- D. parliamentary sovereignty

**Key: B** — Popular sovereignty locates ultimate authority in the people. Legal sovereignty locates it in the constitution or parliament.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q2 (wording repaired)*

**The right of a citizen to seek redress means the right to ______.**

- A. free access to legal materials
- B. appeal against every judgement
- C. approach a court for a remedy
- D. exercise political liberty without restraint

**Key: C** — Seeking redress means asking a court or tribunal to remedy a wrong. It is not a licence to appeal every decision or to act outside the law.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q3 (wording repaired)*

**An order compelling a lower court or public authority to perform its public duty is called ______.**

- A. the great writ
- B. the writ of ombudsman
- C. none of the above
- D. the writ of mandamus

**Key: D** — Mandamus requires a public body to perform a duty it has refused or failed to perform. Habeas corpus concerns unlawful detention; certiorari quashes a decision.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q4 (wording repaired)*

**A form of government whose guiding principle is “rule of the best” is ______.**

- A. aristocracy
- B. democracy
- C. timocracy
- D. plutocracy

**Key: A** — Aristocracy means rule by a small group regarded as the best qualified. Plutocracy is rule by the wealthy and timocracy by property owners.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q5 (wording repaired)*

**Deconcentration of power means ______.**

- A. the sharing of powers among tiers of government
- B. the transfer of administrative authority to central-government officials stationed in the regions
- C. the creation of semi-autonomous units to govern parts of the state
- D. the delegation of powers to component units that already exist in the state

**Key: B** — Deconcentration shifts administrative authority from the capital to field offices of the same central government. Sharing power among tiers is federalism; transferring it to separate units is devolution.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q6 (wording repaired)*

**The independence of the judiciary means that ______.**

- A. the judiciary must not concern itself with legislative or executive affairs
- B. court decisions cannot be reviewed by the executive
- C. legislative and executive influence on judicial decision-making must be prevented
- D. the dignity of judges must be preserved

**Key: C** — Judicial independence protects judges from external pressure so they decide on law and evidence. It does not mean courts never interact with the other arms; judicial review is part of the checks.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q7 (wording repaired)*

**A political party can be said to have a national outlook if ______.**

- A. it is ethnic-based but has branches across the country
- B. it is registered by the electoral commission as a national party
- C. it is led by people who are famous at home and abroad
- D. its membership cuts across ethnic boundaries

**Key: D** — A national party draws members from many ethnic and regional groups. Registration or famous leaders do not by themselves give it a national character.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q9*

**Argument, negotiation and deliberation on the subject matter of a bill begin at the ______.**

- A. second reading
- B. third reading
- C. first reading
- D. committee stage

**Key: A** — The second reading is where the principle of the bill is debated. The first reading is formal introduction, the committee stage examines details, and the third reading is final approval.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q10*

**A vote of no confidence means ______.**

- A. the same thing as impeachment in a presidential system
- B. disapproval of the government which, if carried, requires the ministers and their head to resign
- C. the power of the head of state to dissolve parliament
- D. disapproval of the prime minister for breaching collective responsibility

**Key: B** — In a parliamentary system the executive depends on the legislature’s confidence. If a no-confidence motion passes, the government resigns or seeks a dissolution and election.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q11 (wording repaired)*

**Popular political participation refers to ______.**

- A. the majority of the people holding public office
- B. a large number of voters turning out at elections
- C. the total activities through which citizens actively take part in political life
- D. the efforts of more than half the citizens to join public debate

**Key: C** — Participation covers voting, campaigning, contacting officials, protesting and standing for office — not only election turnout or office-holding.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q13 (wording repaired)*

**The area from which one or more members are elected to a public office is called an electoral ______.**

- A. college
- B. ward
- C. district
- D. constituency

**Key: D** — A constituency is the electoral area represented by an elected member. An electoral college is a body that elects someone indirectly.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q14*

**Citizenship by naturalisation is ______.**

- A. the process by which a non-citizen who satisfies constitutional criteria becomes a legal member of the state
- B. the same as citizenship by marriage and registration
- C. attaining citizenship through honorary means
- D. the process by which an alien receives a certificate from the judiciary and becomes a citizen by birth

**Key: A** — Naturalisation is a legal process: an adult foreigner who meets residence, character and other constitutional conditions applies and takes an oath of allegiance.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q15 (wording repaired)*

**The last measure used by a pressure group when it issues a condition to be met within a fixed time is called ______.**

- A. lobbying
- B. an ultimatum
- C. dialogue
- D. a strike

**Key: B** — An ultimatum is a final demand with a deadline, warning of action if it is not met. Lobbying and dialogue are earlier, softer tactics.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q16 (wording repaired)*

**A form of government organised by a council of military officers is called a ______.**

- A. militocracy
- B. stratocracy
- C. military junta
- D. mobocracy

**Key: C** — A junta is a small group, typically military officers, ruling after seizing power. The council-of-officers form is specifically a junta.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q18*

**The non-stratified society in the pre-colonial era was that of the ______, where decisions were made by ______.**

- A. Yoruba; the Oyomesi
- B. Hausa/Fulani; the Emir
- C. Igbo; the Okpara
- D. Igbo; consensus

**Key: D** — Igbo society was largely acephalous: village assemblies and age grades decided issues by consensus. The Okpara headed the council of elders, but consensus characterised the system.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q19*

**The creation of twelve states was an achievement of the ______ regime.**

- A. Yakubu Gowon
- B. Abdulsalami Abubakar
- C. Ibrahim Babangida
- D. Murtala Mohammed

**Key: A** — Gowon created twelve states in 1967, replacing the four regions. Murtala created seven more in 1976 and Babangida created eleven in 1987 and 1991.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q21 (wording repaired)*

**The basis of Nigeria’s foreign policy during the Independence Constitution era was ______.**

- A. Eurocentric
- B. Afrocentric
- C. neo-colonialist
- D. anti-apartheid

**Key: B** — From independence, Nigeria put Africa at the centre of its foreign policy — decolonisation, African unity and support for liberation movements — while remaining non-aligned in the Cold War.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q22*

**The Brussels Conference of 1889–90 was primarily concerned with ______.**

- A. the scramble for Africa
- B. implementing the decisions of the Berlin Conference
- C. ending the slave trade
- D. planning how to penetrate Africa economically

**Key: C** — The Brussels Anti-Slavery Conference of 1889–90 focused on suppressing the slave trade, especially in Africa. The Berlin Conference (1884–85) dealt with the rules of colonial partition.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q23 (wording repaired)*

**According to Lewis Samuel, imperialism identified with pure conquest and unequivocal exploitation is ______.**

- A. neo-colonialism
- B. progressive imperialism
- C. colonialism
- D. regressive imperialism

**Key: D** — Lewis Samuel Feuer distinguished progressive imperialism, which claims to spread civilisation, from regressive imperialism, which seeks conquest and exploitation for its own sake.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q24 (wording repaired)*

**One major defect of the Nigerian Council that led to its replacement was that it was ______.**

- A. unrepresentative
- B. poorly attended
- C. able to make law only for the Southern Protectorate
- D. less powerful than the Executive Council

**Key: A** — The Nigerian Council (1922) was largely advisory and nominated, with no elected Nigerian members, so it did not represent public opinion. It was replaced under the Richards Constitution.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q25 (wording repaired)*

**An important provision of the 1946 Constitution was that it ______.**

- A. introduced federalism in Nigeria
- B. conceived federalism for Nigeria
- C. introduced an official majority in the Legislative Council for the first time
- D. created the office of head of government at regional level

**Key: B** — The Richards Constitution of 1946 divided Nigeria into three regions and is credited with conceiving a federal structure. Full federalism with regional premiers came later, under Lyttleton (1954).

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q26*

**Implementation of government programmes is the primary duty of the ______, which is part of the ______.**

- A. courts; judiciary
- B. public service; civil service
- C. civil service; public service
- D. ministers; Ministry of Works

**Key: C** — The civil service is the permanent administrative arm that implements policy, and it is one component of the wider public service, which also includes boards, corporations and the armed forces.

**The most recent target plans and agenda of the United Nations are known as the ______.**

- A. Resolution 2020
- B. Millennium Development Goals
- C. Agenda 2063
- D. Sustainable Development Goals

**Key: D** — The Sustainable Development Goals (2015–2030) succeeded the Millennium Development Goals. Agenda 2063 is the African Union’s plan.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q29*

**Under the 1963 Constitution, a federal judge could be removed by ______.**

- A. a two-thirds majority of the National Assembly
- B. the Federal Judicial Service Commission
- C. the Nigerian Judicial Council
- D. the executive president

**Key: A** — The 1963 Constitution required an address supported by a two-thirds majority of the federal legislature before a judge could be removed, protecting the judiciary from unilateral executive action.

**Under the 1999 Constitution, the Chief Law Officer and the head of the Supreme Court are respectively the ______.**

- A. Chief Justice; Chief Judge
- B. Attorney-General; Chief Justice
- C. Minister of Justice; Chief Judge
- D. Chief Registrar; Chief Justice

**Key: B** — The Attorney-General of the Federation is the Chief Law Officer. The Chief Justice of Nigeria heads the Supreme Court.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q31 (wording repaired)*

**The scope of the EFCC’s duties does not extend to ______.**

- A. arresting suspects of cybercrime and online fraud
- B. arresting both the giver and the taker in a corruption case
- C. conducting trials and punishing persons involved in corruption
- D. investigating drug-related money laundering

**Key: C** — The EFCC investigates and prosecutes economic and financial crimes; only courts can try and punish. That separation is itself an aspect of the rule of law.

- A. UPN
- B. NPP
- C. GNPP
- D. NPN

**Key: D** — The National Party of Nigeria (NPN) campaigned on the Green Revolution to boost food production and won the 1979 presidential election.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q35*

**Nigeria’s federalism is often measured against the classical federal principle associated with ______.**

- A. K. C. Wheare
- B. Abraham Lincoln
- C. Thomas Hobbes
- D. Aristotle

**Key: A** — K. C. Wheare defined the federal principle as a division of powers between general and regional governments, each independent within its sphere — the benchmark used to judge Nigeria’s system.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q36 (wording repaired)*

**The most representative organ of the United Nations is the ______.**

- A. Security Council
- B. General Assembly
- C. Economic and Social Council
- D. International Court of Justice

**Key: B** — The General Assembly includes all member states, each with one vote. The Security Council has limited membership and primary responsibility for peace and security.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q37*

**The Executive Council of the African Union makes decisions by ______.**

- A. a one-third majority vote
- B. discretion
- C. consensus
- D. a veto of the General Secretariat

**Key: C** — The AU Executive Council generally proceeds by consensus; where consensus fails, a two-thirds majority of members may decide substantive questions.

*Source: JAMB 2019 Q38*

**Amendment of the United Nations Charter is the duty of the ______.**

- A. Security Council
- B. Secretariat
- C. Trusteeship Council
- D. General Assembly

**Key: D** — Under Article 108, amendments are adopted by a two-thirds vote of the General Assembly and then ratified by member states, including all permanent members of the Security Council.
