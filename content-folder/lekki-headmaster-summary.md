# The Lekki Headmaster — chapter-by-chapter summary (our words)

Kabir Alabi Garba's novel follows Adebepo "Bepo" Adewale, the long-serving principal of Stardom Schools in Lekki, Lagos — nicknamed the Lekki Headmaster after he mimicked the old TV drama *Village Headmaster* and insisted the word is pronounced "PrinciPL". The choice at the heart of the story is simple: his wife Seri (a nurse in London) and his two children Nike and Kike are in England, a teaching post waits for him there, yet he cannot bring himself to leave the students and school he loves. Every chapter circles that decision.

## 1 Dusk…
An ordinary assembly turns into a breakdown. Ikenna Egbu, an SSS student back from a Jos excursion, fires the school up with tales of the Lamingo Dam, Shere Hills, Wase Rocks and the Solomon Lar Amusement Park — and declares he would rather settle in Jos than "Japa-ing to Canada or London". Afterwards, at the podium, Bepo silently drops the microphone, weeps uncontrollably and is led to his office by Vice-Principal Grace Apeh, nurse Mrs Titi and the CRK teacher, Oyelana. The MD (proprietor) Mrs Ibidun Gloss is called; the physics teacher and pastor Oye Wande attempts gentle persuasion; international calls to Seri go unanswered; the MD reluctantly sends him home. Background detail: a recent boarding fee cut (₦250,000 → ₦165,000) transformed punctuality; the WASSCE 90% success already earned teachers ₦20,000 (₦30,000 for distinction subjects — Obong Ukaku and Taye Kareem for Chemistry and Geography Ds; any F9 means sacking, as with the MD's cousin Funso Daniels). The assembly prays with the second stanza of the National Anthem on Tuesdays/Thursdays.

## 2 The Enticement
After about five days of avoidance and questioning, Bepo admits it: he wants to emigrate to the UK. His reasons are family (Seri in London, children already settled — Nike at university, Kike in secondary), an agreed teaching post (≈ £3,600 a month) versus his ₦400,000 Stardom salary (~₦6 million/month at the then exchange rate — Audu mocks the math in the staff room, Oyelana counters that Seri will face pound-priced living). He is torn because he loves the learners and hates leaving a developing nation to serve an already developed one. Retirement at 55 was four years away; he studies business seminars (water, transport, possibly a school) — his small school Fruitful Future had collapsed after NYSC; he teaches English but trained in English/History at University of Benin.

## 3 Migration Tales
Bepo catalogues what Nigerians living abroad tell him — and the failures too. A friend in the USA quotes $150–$250/day work, up to four days/week, ≈ $2,400/month; the Yoruba proverb `oga ta, oga o ta, owo alaaru a pe` (however the employer performs, the worker still takes the full pay). Sola, the former Home Economics teacher at Stardom, now in Manchester with a husband pursuing a Master's, describes marrying rent (£650/month non-furnished), the 30-minute rail to London, the fact that children's education and 911 emergency response are effectively free, and that her children all get free schooling — while spouses often only pass each other by shift-working. She warns married migrants that funding breakdowns destroy plans. Jare, a banker who left, burst into tears caring for an old couple; Hope's wife stopped funding his Master's; Riike from the USA bought two houses in Ibadan inside three years; Akindele (55), an 18-year-married divorcee, still works corrections for 12 straight hours, pitying fellow retirees; Chief Waliem, a heavyweight, saw three children drop out when banks seized assets. 3,000 doctors migrate yearly; cooperative defaults (Mr Nku vanished with his ₦2 million loan); a driver tried to sell a school bus for his son abroad.

## 4 A Case of Visa Denied
Late at night Bepo gets a call from Mrs Ignatius, an oil-firm executive's wife whose husband Ibe wanted to relocate the family to London. The visa was denied after a DNA test showed Favour (15, SS2) was not Ibe's genetic child. Ibe has left the house; the wife fears he may never return. Her son Lyi is beginning to suspect the DNA reason. A parallel episode surfaces: Mrs Mary Ladele and son Bibi live a shortened view — Bibi dreams of attack by Mr Wala-Ayesoro (the government teacher, deep facial marks); the family complains; Bibi is moved houses; Ayesoro disappears to the property wing (Stardom Hub). Bepo toys with writing a memoir, "protecting his peace" behind a wall of seniority.

## 5 Snake in the Roof
A lunch-time walk lets the MD discover that the back-gate land (acquired two years before) is stuffed with teachers' cars (Toyota, Hyundai, Venza, Mazda) and two buses. Fearing embezzlement, she demands answers from the accountant, Jeremi Amos, and Bepo. The Stardom cooperative financed the fleet: N95 million in reserve, over N50 million loaned. Her mother, Chief Mrs Solape Bayo, chairs a family board (siblings Martins and Oye Bayo): new rules cap borrowing at ₦250,000, require the MD's approval for each loan, and inform management of the cooperative's elections — fearing staff might revolt and found their own school with the funds.

## 6 Ade as Well as Jide Comes vs. Come
At Open Day (best-dressed award, parent gifts for the Gbayi family; school hides embarrassments until a parent erupts), the MD summons Bepo. Mr Guta — father of Dorah (SS2) and Nicholas (JSS2) — saw one line in Fafore's notes: "Ade as well as Jide comes early". She threatens dismissal; Bepo objects, citing the as-well-as singular rule — 'Ade as well as Jide comes early' is correct English. The whole staff checks online; Fafore and Bepo are vindicated; the MD is publicly humiliated; Audu mock-pardons everyone. Interwoven flashback: Fafore, worn thin by distance (Ogun State commute, 4am wake, 4:30am house, 6am arrival, ₦175,000 salary after 22 years), is absolved. Bepo remembers being pilloried as "Oga Tisa", an unpaid-youth-without-savings traumatic episode (the ₦2,500 electricity-tariff fraud, the Iya Mathew elubo-dunking) that he knows firsthand what a jobless life does to a man.

## 7 Ritualists
Years earlier at Beesway Group of School (outskirts of Lagos), Bepo, as senior English teacher, lobbied the director, Mr Egi Meko, against the grammatically incorrect name "Group of School". Meko refused — citing divine inspiration, CAC registration, ministry approval. Dragged into a Parent–Teacher Association meeting, Meko warned Bepo off. One night Bepo gets up at 2:30am, finds the floodlights dead, storms outside with a crucifix and a machete to find five men with a large cow — live burial. A man hits his wrist with a club; others beat him; the director arrives and orders leniency. Later Bepo declines anyway to leave quietly. Ten years earlier he had refused Mr Ogo (a parent), who offered a ₦35,000 grain-sprinkling ritual for boundless enrolment; his school Fruitful Future failed from road collapse and a burst pipe regardless. Ten years after Beesway, on TV, Ogo is revealed as a murderer of an infertility client (claiming ₦29m) — inflicted pit-death. Bepo cannot decide whether to report the Beesway ritual; the director apologises that it was "a special prayer for his late father, who gave the land". Bepo silently packs and leaves.

## 8 Missions Unaccomplished
Prefect elections follow a ritual: interest forms (₦50,000 Head Boy/Girl; ₦40,000 deputies; ₦25,000 others), panel screening, students' speeches at Speech Day, voting; a debtor rule administered by the MD — "If you want a debtor to lead fee payers, ask a tenant to lead the landlord." Speech Day slips: Banky (SS2, Social Prefect candidate) tells the hall Tosh is the son of an ex-convict; Tosh (also SS2) storms out. They had rivalled since JSS3 (3–2 hip-hop dance vote loss); their mothers duelled at PTA (Banky's mother won treasurer); their fathers are politicians — Progressives All Congress and Democratic People's Party. Tosh's father — Chief Didi Ogba, senior lawyer and politician whose detention over an alleged ₦2.5bn misappropriation ended at release (company ordered refunded; not directly guilty) — demands published apologies; Banky's parents say the video shows no name drop; the school tries alternative dispute resolution. Bepo leaves before the resolution and before the five-year "Breath Project" (invention club, phone-making from recycled parts, funded by Life Grid NGO) matures — hoping to fly back for the launch.

## 9 Laughing Waterfalls
Despite complaints, Stardom deserves praise for exposure: an excursion per term within Nigeria, one annual international trip, celebrations of Children's Day, Mother's Day and Teachers' Day, plus concerts and experts' lectures. Sites include the Ikogosi Warm Springs (1852, discovery by Rev. John S. McGee), Erin-Ijesa (Olumirin) Waterfalls, Owu Waterfalls in Kwara (highest in West Africa at 120m, 330ft escarpment), Gurara Falls in Niger State, Yankari, the War Museum, the Hanging Lake, Kano Palace, Ooni of Ife's palace and Olumo Rock lore ("Ile la tii ke'eko rode"), Obafemi Awolowo University (the MD's alma mater), Calabar Carnival and Ofala Festival. Lagos's own landmarks are itineraries too. A 40-student JSS/SSS Badagry trip, personally led by Bepo, is the novel's pivot: the king explains that the town was founded in the late 1720s by Popo fugitives fleeing Dahomey's Fon wars, that its contact with white traders seeded the palm-oil port, and that the First Storey Building (Henry Townsend, 1846) housed the first Yoruba-Bible Bible-translation base, now hosting the Black Heritage Museum — where Bepo learns the slave trade ran 1500 to 1900 and begins to wonder whether "Japa", voluntary migration to the former masters' countries, is not a modern form of bondage he is preparing to join.

## 10 Passport Pains
With the pandemic and tales of hassle, Bepo lets his passport expire for two years. By early 2022 family pressure makes it an emergency. He skirts notorious Ikoyi and explores other routes; eventually chooses Agodi Gate, Ibadan (Oyo State), because crowds are smaller. A tout, Tai — an ordinary business-centre operator working in cahoots with immigration officials — charges ₦100,000 for a 10-year (64-page) passport versus the official price, plus ₦2,000 tip. He finishes data capturing within two hours; a NIN officer's validation visit stretches another three weeks because of network glitches. On the way there he recalls J.P. Clark's poem 'Ibadan' — "running splash of rust and gold... seven hills like broken china in the sun". Time pressures spike, but he keeps the appointment.

## 11 Point of No Return
The farewell runs three days. Wednesday: a novelty match featuring former governor... (fine details: referee with outlandish outfits baffles players; penalties awarded five times; a national soccer trialist saves for Stardom). Friday: a debate on "creativity over science"; the arts team wins on development-gap grounds — culture, music, Nollywood, pride, creativity, exports, and Nigeria never having won a Nobel Prize (whereas Wole Soyinka did for Literature, Chinua Achebe did not, the OCR text is garbled but reference frame intact). Cultural programme: skit of Bepo ("PrinciPL" catchphrase, the 'other way...' stage voice, "if you say education is too expensive..."); Koroso (Hausa), Atilogwu (Igbo), Bata (Yoruba); then the Canoe dance of Badagry (trained by the National Troupe) transports him — he blurts "Noooo!", caught in a dream. The MD praises him: employed by her late father, Chief David Aje, 24 years ago; resumed eight days late but never betrayed trust; skill, drive, empathy, morals — the pillars. He gets a domiciliary cheque between ₦10 billion joke-figure and the real figure of "not less than $10,000" — the largest ever given to a retiring/disengaging Stardom staff. He weeps openly.

## 12 Dawn
Determined to beat the 10:00pm Emirates flight (flashback: British Airways trip he missed by an hour), Bepo leaves home by 3:00pm; his landlord Ogunwale drives him in a Honda Pilot; Seri calls hectoring him to recheck Seri's advice (checking ticket twice); her request list for Oyingbo-skipped iru (locust beans), egusi, ground crayfish, dry snail — Kemi's "Japa" quip lands ("Mr Bepo go to London!"). Sold Pathfinder SUV for ₦15m to Amos; deep freeze and electronics gifted; check-in at 5:30pm; seat 56, gate 2A; forgot phone in jacket pocket — sigh of relief that nothing was missed; half-sleeps into a dream in the Heritage Museum; counts "seven million" slaves; white man says "Enter!"; he screams "Noooo!" — wakes. Plane takes off 10:45pm. Monday, the school gasps at his absence; the VP cannot quite fill his shoes; the MD speaks of a future principal — then shouts of "Principoo!" reverberate at the gate. Bepo has quietly returned. "My heart is here... to complete my mission!" Students sweep him through assembly to the school anthem: "We're Stars, we're Stars! Yet Stardom Schools will win!"

## Characters (uncompressed leitmotifs)
- **Bepo Adewale** — English/History graduate of University of Benin; ~24 years at Stardom; principal, Stardom Kiddies (4 years as head), mentor to everyman; tic: hands in left pocket, dour voice, emotional transparency; values: school = home.
- **Seri** (wife) — nurse in London; household head/central London lobbyist; uses the children to press for relocation; voices migration reality.
- **Nike and Kike** — two children in England; benefit from free UK primary/secondary; Nike will pay fees at university.
- **Mrs Ibidun Gloss** — MD/proprietor; law graduate of Obafemi Awolowo University; chronic sitting pain; blunt, funny, second generation (mother Chief Mrs Solape Bayo); announce job like a soap.
- **Grace Apeh** — VP; steady, diplomatic, calm; acts when Bepo is unfit.
- **Mr Audu** — First Arts teacher; satirical; mock-pardon; fantasises about emigration but is nobody's marching orders.
- **Justus Anabel** — Chemistry teacher asked to conclude assembly that fateful morning.
- **Mr Fafore** — English teacher in hardship; Ogun State self-builder; double Punctuality-award winner; almost sacked for an acceptable sentence.
- **Chief Didi Ogba** — Tosh's father; top lawyer/politician.
- **Mr Egi Meko** — Beesway director; refusal of the Group-of-Schools correction; alleged occult tastes.
- **Tai** — Ibadan tout posing as a consultant.
- **Sola, Jare, Hope, Riike, Akindele** — the diaspora chorus; **Ikenna Egbu**, **Bibi**, **Favour**, **Banky**, **Tosh** — pupils.

## Themes in our words
Teaching as patriotic service vs migration as material salvation; family duty vs community duty; leadership as stewardship (succession, calendar, Breath Project), honesty vs shortcut, Nigeria as a place of value and attachment (excursions consecrate belonging), perverse wealth (cooperative → flight), ritualistic deception vs patient work, and public mourning/celebration (initiation, farewell, return) as communal identity.

## Literary techniques
Framing (opening on tears, closing on return), episodic structure (each chapter a vignette that tightens the knot), humour as moral pressure release (mock trials, witcher jokes, nicknames), symbol (passport, ticket, gate, the canoe dance, the slave museum), mirrored times (2:30am vs 10:45pm), Yoruba proverbs as cultural narrative voice, first-person irony (jura), and circular structure (dusk → dawn).
