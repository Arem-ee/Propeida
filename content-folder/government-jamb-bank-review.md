# JAMB Government starter bank — owner review sheet

40 audited questions from the 2020 paper. Eight defective items were rejected (wrong key or broken options). Each question below has one key and a short explanation.


## Constitution and the Rule of Law

**1. An example of a country with an unwritten constitution is ______.**

- A. Nigeria
- B. United Kingdom
- C. U.S.A
- D. Sierra Leone

**Key: B** — The United Kingdom has no single written constitutional document; its constitution is found in statutes, common law and conventions.

**2. The rule of law is only applicable in ______.**

- A. in developing countries
- B. in western type democracies
- C. in states where laws are supreme
- D. in socialist states

**Key: C** — The rule of law requires everyone, including the government, to be subject to the law. It operates where laws are supreme.

**3. The fundamental laws of a country are contained in its ______.**

- A. constitution
- B. party programmes
- C. manifestoes
- D. edicts

**Key: A** — A constitution contains the fundamental laws that establish and limit government.

**4. According to the 1979 Constitution, to be elected president one must have attained the age of ______.**

- A. 21yrs
- B. 50yrs
- C. 35yrs
- D. 65yrs

**Key: C** — The 1979 Constitution set the minimum age for president at 35 years.


## Arms of Government

**1. The legislative arm of government is ______.**

- A. the cabinet
- B. the body that interprets laws
- C. the body that executes laws
- D. the body that makes laws

**Key: D** — The legislature makes laws; the executive implements them and the judiciary interprets them.

**2. An important advantage of a public corporation is that it ______.**

- A. government competes with private enterprises
- B. eliminate private enterprises
- C. makes quick profit at the expense of the people
- D. render crucial services to the people in areas which the civil service cannot effectively handle

**Key: D** — Public corporations deliver essential services in areas where the civil service or private firms cannot operate effectively.

**3. A bill becomes an Act of Parliament after it has been ______.**

- A. Signed by the head of state
- B. debated in the house
- C. processed through the committee of the house
- D. passed by the parliament

**Key: A** — A bill becomes an Act after the head of state assents to it. Debate, committee work and passage are earlier stages.

**4. Under Nigeria's 1999 Constitution, the power to nominate members of the judiciary at the federal level is vested in the ______.**

- A. supreme court
- B. Nigeria Bar Association
- C. Federal Judicial Service Commission
- D. National Judicial Council

**Key: C** — The Federal Judicial Service Commission nominates federal judicial officers for appointment.

**5. The supremacy of the legislature exists in ______.**

- A. France
- B. Britain
- C. Nigeria
- D. Soviet Union

**Key: B** — In Britain Parliament is supreme: the legislature can make or unmake any law.

**6. The executive checks the excesses of the legislature through ______.**

- A. the ratification of treaties
- B. assent to bills
- C. issuance of orders
- D. the use of veto power

**Key: D** — The executive veto lets the president reject bills passed by the legislature.


## Pre-colonial and Colonial Administration

**1. The Crown Colony of Lagos was created in ______.**

- A. 1914
- B. 1861
- C. 1960
- D. 1900

**Key: B** — Lagos was annexed as a Crown Colony in 1861.

**2. The Lyttleton Constitution is important because it ______.**

- A. introduced franchise to Nigerian politics
- B. abolished the principle of indirect rule
- C. confirmed Nigeria as a federal structure
- D. made Nigeria a sovereign nation

**Key: C** — The Lyttleton Constitution of 1954 confirmed Nigeria as a federation with regional premiers and a division of powers.

**3. The British Government revoked the charter of the Royal Niger Company and took over the direct administration of Nigeria in ______.**

- A. 1861
- B. 1906
- C. 1900
- D. 1915

**Key: C** — In 1900 Britain revoked the Royal Niger Company charter and took direct control of Nigeria.

**4. The Galadima in the emirate system was ______.**

- A. A Judge
- B. Administrator of the capital
- C. Village head
- D. Officer in charge of the army

**Key: B** — The Galadima administered the capital of an emirate under the Emir.

**5. The most senior member of the council of elders in the Igbo political system was the ______.**

- A. Obi
- B. Eze
- C. Okpara
- D. Ofo

**Key: C** — The Okpara, the oldest man in the village, headed the Igbo council of elders.

**6. The traditional political system of the Yoruba kingdom was ______.**

- A. Monarchical
- B. Egalitarian
- C. Republican
- D. Democratic

**Key: A** — Yoruba kingdoms were monarchical, with an Oba at the head of each kingdom.

**7. The nationalist movements in Nigeria and the Gold Coast (Ghana) ______.**

- A. produced the same result in both countries.
- B. led by philosopher-kings
- C. dominated by traditional rulers
- D. influenced each other significantly

**Key: D** — Nationalist movements in Nigeria and the Gold Coast influenced each other significantly.

**8. The Ogboni cult in the pre-colonial Yoruba political system played the role of ______.**

- A. Executive.
- B. Police
- C. Judiciary
- D. Legislature

**Key: C** — The Ogboni cult performed judicial and religious functions in the pre-colonial Yoruba system.

**9. All the following ethnic groups in Northern Nigeria had their pre-colonial political structure influenced by Islamic culture except ______.**

- A. TIV
- B. Kanuri
- C. Fulani
- D. Nupe

**Key: A** — The Tiv were not significantly influenced by Islamic culture, unlike the Kanuri, Fulani and Nupe.

**10. The motion for Nigeria's independence was moved by ______.**

- A. Chief Obafemi Awolowo
- B. Chief Anthony Enahoro
- C. Dr. Nnamdi Azikiwe
- D. Sir Ahmadu Bello

**Key: B** — Chief Anthony Enahoro moved the motion for independence in 1953.

**11. Indirect rule in Nigeria encouraged ______.**

- A. exploitation and oppression
- B. inter-communal cooperation
- C. rise of nationalism
- D. communal integration

**Key: C** — Indirect rule exposed Nigerians to colonial exploitation and helped spark the rise of nationalism.


## Citizenship

**1. All these are civil obligations of every Nigerian citizen except ______.**

- A. obedience to law
- B. freedom of conscience
- C. respect for the national anthem and flag
- D. payment of tax.

**Key: B** — Freedom of conscience is a civil liberty, not an obligation. Obedience to law, respect for national symbols and payment of tax are duties.


## Foreign Policy and International Organisations

**1. Which of these international organisations was the predecessor of the United Nations?**

- A. The North Atlantic Treaty Organisation
- B. The League of Nations
- C. The European economic Community
- D. The organization of American States

**Key: B** — The League of Nations (1920–1946) was the predecessor of the United Nations.

- A. supply of arms to Rhodesia
- B. negative utterances of Nigeria
- C. support for UNITA rebels in Angola
- D. failure to impose sanctions on South Africa's apartheid government

**Key: D** — Nigeria withdrew from the 1986 Edinburgh Commonwealth Games to protest Britain's failure to impose sanctions on apartheid South Africa.

**3. The Nigerian who once served as President of the United Nations General Assembly was ______.**

- A. Maitama Sule
- B. Joseph Garba
- C. Ibrahim Gambari
- D. Arthur Mbanefo

**4. The Nigeria–Cameroon crisis over the Bakassi Peninsula was subject to the decision of ______.**

- B. Maroua Accord.
- C. decision of the ECOWAS tribunal
- D. decision of the International Court of Justice

**Key: D** — The International Court of Justice decided the Bakassi Peninsula dispute in favour of Cameroon in 2002.

**5. The country that withdrew her membership of ECOWAS is ______.**

- A. Guinea Bissau
- B. Burkina Faso
- C. Mauritania

**6. Nigeria is a member of the following international organisations except ______.**

- A. ECOWAS
- B. NATO
- C. Commonwealth
- D. OPEC

**Key: B** — Nigeria belongs to ECOWAS, the Commonwealth and OPEC, but not NATO.

**7. The review of Nigeria's foreign policy under the Murtala/Obasanjo regime was done by ______.**

- A. Adedeji commission
- B. Phillips commission
- C. Aboyade commission
- D. Udoji Commission

**Key: A** — The Adedeji Commission reviewed Nigeria’s foreign policy under the Murtala/Obasanjo regime.


## Forms and Systems of Government

**1. The major difference between the presidential and parliamentary systems is ______.**

- A. Judicial independence
- B. Party system
- C. separation of power
- D. the passage of bills

**Key: C** — The presidential system separates the executive from the legislature; the parliamentary system fuses them, and the executive depends on parliamentary confidence.

**2. A system of government ruled by old people is known as ______.**

- A. Theocracy
- B. Gerontocracy
- C. Autocracy
- D. Aristocracy

**Key: B** — Gerontocracy is rule by the elderly.


## Nigerian Federalism and Military Rule

**1. The 1976 local government reforms were a benefit to ______.**

- A. the civil service
- B. the federal government
- C. the local government
- D. the police

**Key: C** — The 1976 reforms gave local governments a uniform structure and direct federal funding, strengthening the third tier.

**2. In the modern local government structure in Nigeria, traditional rulers play ______.**

- A. A judicial role
- B. A legislative role
- C. An advisory role
- D. An executive role

**Key: C** — Traditional rulers advise modern local governments; they do not legislate or execute.

**3. Military intervention in Nigeria was a result of ______.**

- A. international pressure for change
- B. perceived incapability of civilians to govern
- C. civilians desire to relinquish power
- D. the desire for a military government.

**Key: B** — Military coups were justified by the perceived inability of civilian governments to govern effectively.

**4. Nigeria's first executive president was ______.**

- A. Alhaji Shehu Shagari
- B. Dr. Nnamdi Azikiwe
- C. Alhaji Tafawa Balewa
- D. Gen. Yakubu Gowon

**Key: A** — Shehu Shagari became Nigeria’s first executive president in 1979.

**5. Which of the following states were created in 1996?**

- A. Gombe and Ekiti
- B. Ebonyi and Osun
- C. Adamawa and Kogi
- D. Akwa Ibom and Zamfara

**Key: A** — Gombe and Ekiti were among the six states created in 1996.


## Political Parties and Elections

**1. A vote taken by the citizenry of a country on an important political issue is a ______.**

- A. referendum
- B. general election
- C. bye-election
- D. recall

**Key: A** — A referendum is a direct vote by the electorate on a specific political issue.


## Structures of Governance

**1. A major feature of a confederal state is that ______.**

- A. political stability is usually low
- B. there is supremacy of the constitution
- C. The center overrides the regions
- D. The citizens often owe allegiance to the center

**Key: A** — Confederal states are often unstable because the centre depends on the goodwill of the constituent units.


## Political Ideologies

**1. The system of government where power derives from total control of the instruments of force is ______.**

- A. fascism
- B. monarchy
- C. capitalism
- D. oligarchy

**Key: A** — Fascism relies on total control of the instruments of force and subordinates the individual to the state.

**2. The development of a classless society is the goal of ______.**

- A. feudalism
- B. conservatism
- C. Marxism
- D. liberalism

**Key: C** — Marxism aims at a classless, communist society.
