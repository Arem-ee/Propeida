# Biology depth tier: first five lessons

Date: 3 November 2026
Scope: *Fungi*, *Plant Divisions*, *Invertebrate Phyla*, *Insects and Their
Importance* and *Vertebrate Classes* in
`content-folder/biology-curriculum.json`.

This is the first Biology depth tier. It fixes the three limits recorded after
the A.2/A.3 audit:

1. life histories were summary traces with no named stages;
2. warning colouration in mushrooms was hedged without saying what the syllabus
   expects;
3. Biology had no optional depth.

## 1. What was added

Each lesson now has three optional sections headed `Go deeper: ...`, placed
after the worked example and before the guided question. The core is unchanged
except for the warning-colouration card in *Adaptive Colouration*.

| Lesson | Depth sections | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Fungi | Rhizopus life cycle; digestion outside the body; why fungi are not plants | 609 |
| Plant Divisions | Alternation of generations; pollen to seed; reading the plant kingdom | 614 |
| Invertebrate Phyla | Taenia life cycle; symmetry, coeloms and segmentation; movement and feeding | 611 |
| Insects and Their Importance | Metamorphosis stage by stage; breathing without lungs; mouthparts and damage | 625 |
| Vertebrate Classes | Frog metamorphosis; heart and circulation; eggs and the move to land | 619 |

The standard asks for 600-900 words of depth in at least two sections. These
lessons use three sections of roughly 190-220 words each, which also satisfies
the 180-300 word per-section guidance.

## 2. What each section adds beyond the core

### Fungi

- **Rhizopus life cycle.** Spore, germination, hypha, mycelium, stolon,
  rhizoid, sporangiophore, sporangium, sporangiospore, zygospore and germ
  sporangium. The core names spores and sporangia; depth names the stages and
  explains the sexual cycle.
- **Digestion outside the body.** Extracellular amylase, protease and lipase;
  absorption across the wall; the moisture, food and oxygen requirements; and
  the saprophyte, parasite and mutualist split.
- **Why fungi are not plants.** Chitin versus cellulose, glycogen versus starch,
  no chlorophyll, hyphal body, fruiting body versus mycelium, yeasts and spores,
  and the bacterium/fungus/plant discrimination.

### Plant Divisions

- **Alternation of generations.** Haploid gametophyte and diploid sporophyte;
  moss protonema, antheridia, archegonia and dependent sporophyte; fern sorus,
  prothallus and free-living gametophyte.
- **Pollen to seed.** Pollen grain as male gametophyte, ovule as female
  gametophyte, pollination versus fertilisation, pollen tube, double
  fertilisation, endosperm, ovule-to-seed and ovary-to-fruit.
- **Reading the plant kingdom.** The discrimination chain from thallophyte to
  bryophyte to pteridophyte to spermatophyte, gymnosperm versus angiosperm, and
  the Spirogyra and rhizoid traps.

### Invertebrate Phyla

- **Taenia life cycle.** Scolex, proglottid, gravid proglottid, egg, oncosphere,
  cysticercus, pork intermediate host and human infection; *T. solium* versus
  *T. saginata*; human cysticercosis.
- **Symmetry, coeloms and segmentation.** Radial versus bilateral, two versus
  three germ layers, acoelomate, pseudocoelomate and coelomate, and why
  segmentation is an advance.
- **Movement and feeding.** Hydra nematocysts, Taenia absorption, Ascaris
  pseudocoelomic movement, earthworm hydrostatic skeleton and setae, arthropod
  jointed limbs and moulting, and the molluscan foot and radula.

### Insects and Their Importance

- **Metamorphosis stage by stage.** Instars, ecdysis, pupa, histolysis and
  histogenesis, ecdysone, juvenile hormone, and the complete/incomplete split.
- **Breathing without lungs.** Spiracles, tracheae, taenidia, tracheoles,
  abdominal pumping, spiracle closure and the diffusion limit on body size.
- **Mouthparts and damage.** Biting-chewing, piercing-sucking, siphoning,
  sponging and chewing-lapping; the pests and vectors each predicts; and the
  control method that follows.

### Vertebrate Classes

- **Frog metamorphosis.** Egg, tadpole, external and internal gills, hind then
  fore limbs, tail resorption, gut shortening, thyroxine and the adult.
- **Heart and circulation.** Two-chambered fish heart with single circulation,
  three-chambered amphibian and reptile hearts with mixing, four-chambered bird
  and mammal hearts with double circulation.
- **Eggs and the move to land.** External fertilisation in water, jelly-covered
  amphibian eggs, the amniotic egg and its four membranes, the shell, incubation
  and the mammalian placenta and milk.

## 3. Warning colouration

The syllabus requires warning colouration in mushrooms. The old card said that
"some brightly coloured mushrooms use warning colouration", which implied a
universal rule the sources do not support. The card now says:

> The syllabus also treats a conspicuous fungal cap as warning colouration, and
> the fly agaric has a bright red cap with pale spots and is toxic, so its
> colour can signal danger.

The bullet adds: "The fly agaric is the syllabus example; a bright cap alone
does not prove toxicity." This keeps the syllabus category, names the standard
example, and avoids claiming that every bright mushroom is poisonous or that
the aposematic function is settled.

The card remains 163 words, inside the 120-180 word budget, and its check still
asks for mimicry using the hoverfly and wasp.
